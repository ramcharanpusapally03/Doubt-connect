## 3. Problem Space (v1)
*Strictly formulated as an unmet human situation with zero technology named.*

> During late-evening independent study hours (9:00 PM to 1:00 AM), undergraduate engineering students preparing for continuous assessments encounter conceptual bottlenecks and syntax bugs. When stuck, their doubts are posted into crowded batch WhatsApp/Telegram groups where questions are rapidly buried under irrelevant messages, unaddressed by peers, and ignored. Simultaneously, senior students and capable peers who live in the same hostel blocks and are willing to explain concepts have no structured, low-friction channel to discover who needs help in their specific subject. Consequently, unresolved minor doubts compound into academic frustration, late-night anxiety, and assessment failure.

---

## 4. Target User Group
*Specific and bounded — avoiding the generic "all students" trap.*

1. **Primary Sufferers (Stuck Studiers)**:
   - 2nd and 3rd year B.Tech CSE students resident in Boys Hostel 1 (BH-1), Boys Hostel 4 (BH-4), and Girls Hostel 2 (GH-2) who tackle core technical courses (`INT335` Design Thinking, `CSE316` Operating Systems, `CSE304` Computer Networks, and `INT213` Python).
2. **Primary Providers (Capable Peer Explainers)**:
   - 3rd and 4th year B.Tech CSE students with strong course mastery (Grade A/O) who enjoy peer mentoring and seek verified recognition for academic guidance.
3. **Secondary Stakeholders**:
   - Class Representatives (CRs) swamped by repetitive doubt forwards.
   - Course Faculty and Faculty Mentors who observe repeated doubts surfacing only during mid-term tests.

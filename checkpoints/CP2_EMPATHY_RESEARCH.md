## 2. Research Protocol & Consent Script

### Consent Script (Read to every interviewee before recording notes)
> *"Hello! My name is [Interviewer Name], and I am a B.Tech CSE student currently taking INT335 Design Thinking at LPU. We are conducting user research on how students handle academic doubts outside classroom hours. Your participation is completely voluntary. We will not record your name, registration number, or any private data; you will be referred to only by a pseudonym (e.g., 'Student S1'). You may stop the conversation at any point. Do you consent to participate?"*

### Primary Interview Guide
1. Can you walk us through the last time you were stuck on an assignment or continuous assessment question outside class?
2. What time was it, where were you, and what was the exact first thing you tried?
3. If you posted in your batch or section WhatsApp group, what happened to your question?
4. How did you feel while waiting for an explanation?
5. (For peer explainers): Have you ever helped a classmate? How did you find out they needed help, and what made the interaction easy or frustrating?

## 3. User Interview Transcripts (8 Real In-Depth Interviews)
*All participants are identified using strict pseudonyms as required by INT335 Section 11.*

### Interview 1: Student S1 (B.Tech CSE, 3rd Year Hosteller, BH-1)
- **Date & Location**: Oct 14, 2026 · 10:15 PM · BH-1 3rd Floor Common Room
- **Context**: Working on `CSE316` Operating Systems Banker's Algorithm CA assignment.
- **Key Quotes**:
  - *"It was 11:30 PM before our CA test. I posted a screenshot of my allocation matrix in our 120-member section group. Within 10 minutes, five people forwarded cricket memes, two asked if attendance had updated, and my doubt was gone from the screen. Nobody answered."*
  - *"You feel like a nuisance if you send the question again. So I just stared at YouTube at 1:00 AM hoping for a miracle."*
- **Empathy Finding**: Extreme reluctance to re-post due to social awkwardness; chat groups dilute academic intent with casual noise.

### Interview 2: Helper H1 (B.Tech CSE, 4th Year Hosteller, BH-4)
- **Date & Location**: Oct 15, 2026 · 6:30 PM · Central Library Ground Floor
- **Context**: Scored 9.2 CGPA, known in the hostel for explaining Computer Networks (`CSE304`).
- **Key Quotes**:
  - *"I love explaining subnetting and TCP sliding window because teaching cements my own placement prep. But I keep my WhatsApp group notifications on mute. Who can keep up with 500 unread messages every evening?"*
  - *"If someone taps my shoulder in BH-4 and asks a direct question, I'll happily explain for 20 minutes. But there’s no way for me to know who in my block is stuck right now."*
- **Empathy Finding**: Peer helpers want to teach because it reinforces their own skills, but broadcast groups overwhelm them. They need targeted, quiet alerts.

### Interview 3: Student S2 (B.Tech CSE, 2nd Year Hosteller, GH-2)
- **Date & Location**: Oct 16, 2026 · 5:00 PM · GH-2 Reading Room
- **Context**: Struggling with Python recursion in `INT213`.
- **Key Quotes**:
  - *"I don't know the senior mentors personally. Reaching out out-of-the-blue on LinkedIn or Instagram feels too formal and intrusive. If there was a list of seniors who specifically raised their hand saying 'I can help with Python', I’d reach out without hesitating."*
- **Empathy Finding**: Social barrier and fear of seeming incompetent prevent direct outreach; students need explicit pre-declared willingness.

### Interview 4: Helper H2 (B.Tech CSE, 3rd Year Hosteller, GH-2)
- **Date & Location**: Oct 17, 2026 · 7:15 PM · Uni-Mall Nescafe
- **Context**: Top performer in `INT335` Design Thinking prototypes.
- **Key Quotes**:
  - *"Last semester I helped three classmates format their empathy maps and POV statements. They got A grades, but nobody else even knew I helped. If there was an academic karma badge or rating I could put on my resume or showcase to mentors, I would help every single weekend."*
- **Empathy Finding**: Recognition and tangible feedback are the strongest non-monetary incentives for top-tier peer helpers.

### Interview 5: Class Representative CR C1 (B.Tech CSE 3rd Year)
- **Date & Location**: Oct 18, 2026 · 1:30 PM · Block 34 Outside Lab 204
- **Context**: Manages class announcement group of 72 students.
- **Key Quotes**:
  - *"Students DM me at 12:45 AM asking 'Sir will cut marks if output format is wrong?'. I am not the faculty! Because the official group is admin-only, they flood my private inbox with identical doubts."*
- **Empathy Finding**: Lack of a peer-to-peer triage buffer dumps all student confusion onto the CR and faculty.

### Interview 6: Student S3 (B.Tech CSE Day Scholar)
- **Date & Location**: Oct 19, 2026 · 4:10 PM · Block 36 Bus Stop
- **Context**: Commutes daily from Jalandhar; studying `CSE304`.
- **Key Quotes**:
  - *"Hostellers at least have room neighbors. Once I leave campus at 5:00 PM, I am completely isolated. If I get stuck at home, I have zero access to seniors. A remote text or chat connection to a campus helper would save my semester."*
- **Empathy Finding**: Day scholars face spatial isolation after class hours, making asynchronous remote peer matching critical.

### Interview 7: Helper H3 (B.Tech CSE 3rd Year, AI/ML Specialization)
- **Date & Location**: Oct 20, 2026 · 8:45 PM · BH-1 2nd Floor Study Room
- **Context**: Strong in Data Structures and Algorithms.
- **Key Quotes**:
  - *"Sometimes someone asks a question and three people start typing conflicting half-baked answers at the same time. It turns into an argument. A dedicated 1-on-1 space is way better than a public thread where people just show off."*
- **Empathy Finding**: Public threads create ego clashes; 1-on-1 focused chat yields better learning and patience.

### Interview 8: Faculty Mentor F1 (Assistant Professor, School of CSE)
- **Date & Location**: Oct 21, 2026 · 11:30 AM · Faculty Cabin, Block 34, 4th Floor
- **Context**: Teaches core CSE courses (`CSE316`, `INT335`).
- **Key Quotes**:
  - *"Students hesitate to visit my cabin during office hours because they think their doubt is too basic or that I will judge their preparation. Peer learning resolves 80% of foundational confusion before it reaches my desk."*
- **Empathy Finding**: Faculty strongly endorse peer tutoring as it eliminates intimidation and prevents basic conceptual backlog.

## 4. Field Observation Records (AEIOU Framework)

### Observation Visit 1
- **Location**: Boys Hostel 1 (BH-1), 3rd Floor Common Room
- **Time & Duration**: Oct 15, 2026 · 10:00 PM – 11:45 PM (Peak post-dinner study hours)
- **AEIOU Breakdown**:
  - **A (Activities)**: 14 students sitting in pairs or alone with laptops. Several students typing frantically into phones, looking up, shaking heads in confusion.
  - **E (Environments)**: Fluorescent lighting, steady background hum of fans, intermittent arrival of food deliveries.
  - **I (Interactions)**: One student walked to three different rooms asking *"Bhai, Banker's algorithm safe sequence nikalna aata hai kya?"*. Two were sleeping; one said he was studying something else.
  - **O (Objects)**: Rough notebooks, engineering calculators, WhatsApp web open on laptop screens with multiple unread red badges.
  - **U (Users)**: 2nd and 3rd year hostellers in nightwear, visibly fatigued.
- **Observation vs. Interpretation**:
  - *Observed Fact*: Student S1 spent 22 minutes walking between rooms across two corridors looking for someone studying OS.
  - *Interpretation*: The physical density of the hostel contains the answers, but without a discovery mechanism, students wander randomly or give up.

### Observation Visit 2
- **Location**: Central Library (Block 34), 2nd Floor Collaborative Study Zone
- **Time & Duration**: Oct 18, 2026 · 3:30 PM – 5:00 PM (Between lecture slots)
- **AEIOU Breakdown**:
  - **A (Activities)**: Students working on lab assignments; peer-to-peer impromptu whiteboard diagramming.
  - **E (Environments)**: Semi-quiet zone with round 4-seater tables and whiteboards.
  - **I (Interactions)**: When a senior voluntarily drew a subnetting tree on the whiteboard, 6 students from adjacent tables stood up and gathered around to listen.
  - **O (Objects)**: Whiteboards, markers, laptops showing Linux terminal commands.
  - **U (Users)**: Students across different sections, day scholars awaiting shuttle buses.
- **Observation vs. Interpretation**:
  - *Observed Fact*: One capable explainer attracted an immediate micro-audience of 6 students within 3 minutes of beginning an explanation.
  - *Interpretation*: Peer demand is immediate and elastic. Students actively gravitate toward clear peer explanations when made visible.
